# TODO — Toybox After Hours

## Repair flow at scale

- The cross-zone scatter is **deliberate** — hunting scattered halves and rejoining them is a pillar of the game alongside sorting, and must not be shortened to make repairs cheaper. Measured on its own terms in `state/tests/replay.rs` (`Strategy::Restorer`), the errand is fully winnable: at the 240-toy shop that is all 28 broken pairs in 56 actions with nothing deferred, ~18s per pair. Cost per pair is unchanged by the shop retune; there are simply fewer pairs. The earlier "zero repairs" figure came from a nearest-first closer, which measures the sorting loop and never runs the errand at all.
- **The scanner gate is softened, not removed.** A carried half now always names the aisle its counterpart landed in ("Other half: head in Checkout"); the scanner upgrades that to distance plus the beacon column ("Scanner: head in Checkout, 17m"). Unaided the errand is a real search of one zone rather than a sweep of the whole shop for one object among hundreds, which was a wall rather than a journey. The scanner's `upgrades.json` description was rewritten to sell what it now actually adds.
- **Mistake penalty and timer are measured now, and neither number needed changing.** A wrong shelf costs 8.0s against a measured 7.7s per toy — a ratio of 1.03, i.e. exactly one more toy's work, which is also the natural cost of having to collect and re-shelve it. `a_wrong_shelf_costs_about_one_toys_worth_of_time` pins the *relationship* rather than the constant, so a retune of shop size, walking speed or tools shows up instead of quietly making the penalty trivial or savage.
- **Decided: identity-derived detail, not 50×2 renderers.** A hundred new models is a great deal of work for an object the player sees for the length of one errand, and they would have to be kept in step with the fifty whole-toy renderers forever. Instead `toys/part_accents.rs` gives each identity a `PartAccent` — a crest shape and scale, a muzzle length, an eye spread — and the ten category renderers read it. Fifty table rows and one `draw_crest` helper. `no_two_identities_in_a_category_break_into_the_same_part` compares only the fields a category actually draws, so a copy-pasted row fails rather than silently shipping two identical halves.

## Game loop and progression

- **Run length is decided: 240 toys, 12 per display.** A shift is ~28 min bare-handed and ~18 min fully equipped, and 13 displays complete against the 11 credits all five tools cost — so the tool economy closes with a little room, and buying tools visibly shortens the run. Measured by `shop_scale_sets_the_length_of_a_shift` (`cargo test --release shop_scale -- --ignored --nocapture`), which sweeps capacity and is the thing to re-run before changing `toy_count` again:

  | capacity | toys | bare-handed | equipped | displays done |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 8 | 160 | 19.1 min | 12.0 min | 14 |
  | **12** | **240** | **27.9 min** | **17.6 min** | **13** |
  | 20 | 400 | 46.8 min | 29.5 min | 14 |
  | 40 | 800 | 92.4 min | 62.1 min | 12 |
  | 100 | 2000 | 228.9 min | 174.0 min | 12 |
  | 200 | 4000 | 456.9 min | 363.5 min | 12 |

  Cost per toy is ~7.8s bare-handed at every size, so length is linear in `toy_count`. **Depth is no longer a constraint** — see the shelf-shadowing entry under Engineering; before that fix this table also carried a shelf-refusal column that went 2, 1133, 7173, 15793 as displays got deeper, and the two largest shops could not be filled at all. Capacity 12 now rests on run length and the credit economy alone: 240 toys is a ~28 minute bare-handed shift, and 13 displays complete against the 11 credits all five tools cost.
- **Deadline and relaxed mode are in.** `shift_seconds` (1800) ends a timed run at `GamePhase::TimeUp`; the title offers *Closing Shift* against the clock and *Relaxed Run* without one, `ShiftMode` persists in the save, and the HUD counts down (amber under 5 min, red under 1). The mistake penalty now bites, because pushing `elapsed_seconds` can end the shift outright — pinned by `state/tests/shift_clock.rs`.
- **The score screen is in** (`ui/score.rs`): grade badge, toys shelved, repairs, wrong shelves, time, and a per-aisle bar table, driven by `GameSession::shift_summary`. Finishing an aisle now announces itself mid-run via `InteractionResult::Placed.completed_zone`. The HUD hides behind the panel so the score is the whole message.
- **The ~88% zone cap is now legible rather than weakened.** `ZoneProgress::broken` counts an aisle's toys currently in halves and `still_to_find()` the rest of its shortfall, so the score screen reads "41 to find - 5 to mend" and the HUD "Plush Corner - 5 to mend / 90%". An aisle with toys in pieces is genuinely not restored, so `is_restored` was left alone; what was wrong was that a player who had shelved every whole toy in an aisle had no way to tell the remainder from toys they had missed. Pinned by `every_aisle_slot_is_accounted_for_as_shelved_broken_or_missing`.
- **A shift is now worth repeating: best runs are recorded.** The game graded you and forgot — nothing carried between shifts by design, since tools are earned and lost inside one run, so a player who cleared 92% had no way to know next time whether they had beaten it. `state/records.rs` keeps a `ShiftRecord` per mode in its own save slot (`records_slot`), separate from the session because "New Game" overwrites that one and a record has to survive exactly that. The score screen reads "Best so far: 154 toys, 2 wrong, 28:32", or "New best" when the run improved it.
  - Better means **more toys, then fewer wrong shelves, then faster** — in that order, so a careful slow clear beats a scrappy quick one and speed only settles a tie. Deliberately not the grade, which is a coarse band that two different runs can share.
  - The title screen shows the record too, above the buttons that pick a mode — by the time the score screen reports one the choice has already been made, and with nothing else carrying between shifts the record is the only reason to pick the clock. Both title captions now sit on a dark plate: 15px text unbacked over a lit shop full of bright toys was barely readable, which applied to the pre-existing mode caption as well.
  - **Records never actually loaded, and the tests all passed.** `load_from_slot_with_migration` hands its callback the whole save wrapper, `{slot, data}`, not the inner payload — and every field of `BestRuns` is `serde(default)`, so deserialising the wrapper straight into it did not fail. Serde ignored the two unknown keys and returned an empty record. Written correctly, silently read back as none, every launch. The fix unwraps the envelope first, exactly as `migrate_save_value` already did for the session save; that one was never affected. Found by `on_disk::a_record_survives_a_round_trip_through_a_file`, written specifically because the disk path was the one link reasoned about rather than observed. The other two on-disk tests cover a missing file (the ordinary first run) and a corrupt one, which must cost the player their records and nothing else.
  - Timed and Relaxed keep **separate** records. A relaxed run has no deadline, so its time is not comparable; pooling them would let an unhurried perfect clear stand as *the* record and leave a Closing Shift player nothing reachable to chase.
- **The deadline is reachable.** `the_deadline_is_reachable_by_a_closer_who_buys_tools` adds `Strategy::Earner` — the only loadout a timed run ever really has, since tools do not carry between shifts — and it clears the shop in 20.0 min against the 30 min deadline, versus 27.9 bare-handed. Ten minutes of slack for a near-optimal closer that teleports in straight lines and never backtracks, so a real player should find 30 minutes comfortable played well and tight played carelessly. Still worth a human playthrough, since the replay's 0.6s-per-interaction constant is untuned.

## Engineering

- **`worst_frame_ms` could not report a number worse than 100.00, so the probe said nothing about stutter.** It was measured from the `dt` handed to `update`, which `main.rs` clamps to 0.1s so one long frame cannot teleport the simulation — a correct clamp for the *simulation*, silently wrong as a *measurement*. A two-second stall and a 100ms hitch read identically. The bench now takes the unclamped `get_frame_time()`, skips the first 60 frames (shader compilation and first-draw costs are startup, not stutter — and one 90ms opening frame otherwise set the figure for the whole run and hid everything after it), and counts `slow_frames` over 16.7ms alongside the worst.
  - **This was the check the LOD change needed and could not get.** Raising `toy_lod_distance` 5 → 8 draws far more geometry, which is exactly the change that could introduce hitches. A/B over four runs each: `slow_frames` is **4–5 per 10 seconds at both settings**, and the worst frame is 34–42ms at both. The extra geometry costs average throughput and nothing else — the hitches are independent of render load.
  - What those hitches *are* is unattributed. Across 5/20/30-second runs the count went 3/6/16, which is neither flat nor cleanly linear, and this machine was compiling throughout. It is a baseline to compare against on a quiet machine, not a verdict — and specifically **not** evidence of a game-internal periodic stall, which is what I would have concluded from the 10-second runs alone.
- **Every toy past five metres was a coloured cube, and the middle LOD band was dead code.** `draw_loose_toys` picks a stand-in cube beyond `toy_lod_distance`, a full *upright* model beyond `toy_pose_distance`, and a full posed model closer than that — but the middle arm is an `else if`, and both distances were **5.0**. Setting them equal deletes that band silently, so the shop rendered as posed models within 5m and boxes everywhere else. The LOD was tuned when the shop held 4000 toys; it holds 240 now, and nothing revisited it after that retune. `toy_lod_distance` is now **8.0**, which makes the upright band live and turns the mid-field from a scatter of boxes into recognisable dragons, bears and blocks.
  - **The perf probe could not have caught this, or anything else.** `TOYBOX_BENCH_SECONDS` reported 59.5 fps on every run because it was vsync-capped — it was measuring the monitor, so a regression would only show once the game was *already* dropping frames. `window_conf` now sets `swap_interval = 0` for a bench run only (the earliest hook there is, hence `game::bench_seconds()` being read from both places). - Balance the five tools against measured pacing. Over a whole 240-toy shift: bare-handed 27.9 min, trolley+scanner 20.5, fully equipped 17.6 — so a full toolset is worth about 37% of the run. The trolley now clearly pays (it used to measure *worse* than bare hands); the speed tools stack on top.
- The replay closer used to walk every toy to the **first** display of its category, ignoring the other three. That fills a quarter of the shop and then spins, carrying each remaining toy to a shelf that has been full for hours and dropping it again — 9700 wasted actions in an 8000-action shift. Every "the shop is unfinishable" number before this was measuring that, not the game. `home_display_index` now picks the nearest matching display *with room*, and `the_closer_clears_the_floor_without_misshelving` asserts the run ends because it ran out of work rather than out of budget.
- **Placement now runs through real aiming** (`aim_and_place`): the closer walks to a gap, faces it, and presses `E` only once `interaction_preview` says it will shelve — so a run is charged for putting each toy away, not just for finding it. With a trolley that is a separate walk per toy in the armful, because the slot just filled is no longer the one the crosshair offers. Doing this uncovered two `interact` ordering bugs and the shelf shadowing below.
- **A full front row used to shadow every row behind it.** Slots sit five to a row, so a display's capacity is its depth, and `targeted_empty_display_slot` took the best-scoring slot and then asked whether it happened to be free. Scoring favours the nearest, so once row one filled, standing at the shelf offered a taken slot and `E` refused with two empty rows a few centimetres further back. It now searches free slots only — holding a toy, the intent is unambiguous, so an occupied slot is not a candidate. Bare-handed shelf refusals went to zero at every capacity, and the 4000-toy shop went from 146 toys shelved and 0 displays completed to 3521 and 12. `a_full_front_row_does_not_block_the_rows_behind_it` pins the rule; `a_display_stays_fillable_to_its_back_row` keeps watching the shipped config.
