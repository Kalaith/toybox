# TODO — Toybox After Hours

## Repair flow at scale

- The cross-zone scatter is **deliberate** — hunting scattered halves and rejoining them is a pillar of the game alongside sorting, and must not be shortened to make repairs cheaper. Measured on its own terms in `state/tests/replay.rs` (`Strategy::Restorer`), the errand is fully winnable: at the 240-toy shop that is all 28 broken pairs in 56 actions with nothing deferred, ~18s per pair. Cost per pair is unchanged by the shop retune; there are simply fewer pairs. The earlier "zero repairs" figure came from a nearest-first closer, which measures the sorting loop and never runs the errand at all.
- **The scanner gate is softened, not removed.** A carried half now always names the aisle its counterpart landed in ("Other half: head in Checkout"); the scanner upgrades that to distance plus the beacon column ("Scanner: head in Checkout, 17m"). Unaided the errand is a real search of one zone rather than a sweep of the whole shop for one object among hundreds, which was a wall rather than a journey. The scanner's `upgrades.json` description was rewritten to sell what it now actually adds.
- **Mistake penalty and timer are measured now, and neither number needed changing.** A wrong shelf costs 8.0s against a measured 7.7s per toy — a ratio of 1.03, i.e. exactly one more toy's work, which is also the natural cost of having to collect and re-shelve it. `a_wrong_shelf_costs_about_one_toys_worth_of_time` pins the *relationship* rather than the constant, so a retune of shop size, walking speed or tools shows up instead of quietly making the penalty trivial or savage.
- **Decided: identity-derived detail, not 50×2 renderers.** A hundred new models is a great deal of work for an object the player sees for the length of one errand, and they would have to be kept in step with the fifty whole-toy renderers forever. Instead `toys/part_accents.rs` gives each identity a `PartAccent` — a crest shape and scale, a muzzle length, an eye spread — and the ten category renderers read it. Fifty table rows and one `draw_crest` helper. `no_two_identities_in_a_category_break_into_the_same_part` compares only the fields a category actually draws, so a copy-pasted row fails rather than silently shipping two identical halves.

## Game loop and progression

- **Run length is decided: 240 toys, 12 per display.** A shift is ~28 min bare-handed and ~18 min fully equipped, and 13 displays complete against the 11 credits all five tools cost — so the tool economy closes with a little room, and buying tools visibly shortens the run. Measured by `shop_scale_sets_the_length_of_a_shift` (`cargo test --release shop_scale -- --ignored --nocapture`), which sweeps capacity and is the thing to re-run before changing `toy_count` again:

  | capacity | toys | bare-handed | equipped | displays done |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 8 | 160 | 19.1 min | 12.0 min | 14 |
  | **12** | **240** | **27.9 min** | **17.6 min** | **13** |
  | 20 | 400 | 46.8 min | 29.5 min | 14 |
  | 40 | 800 | 92.4 min | 62.1 min | 12 |
  | 100 | 2000 | 228.9 min | 174.0 min | 12 |
  | 200 | 4000 | 456.9 min | 363.5 min | 12 |

  Cost per toy is ~7.8s bare-handed at every size, so length is linear in `toy_count`. **Depth is no longer a constraint** — see the shelf-shadowing entry under Engineering; before that fix this table also carried a shelf-refusal column that went 2, 1133, 7173, 15793 as displays got deeper, and the two largest shops could not be filled at all. Capacity 12 now rests on run length and the credit economy alone: 240 toys is a ~28 minute bare-handed shift, and 13 displays complete against the 11 credits all five tools cost.
- **Deadline and relaxed mode are in.** `shift_seconds` (1800) ends a timed run at `GamePhase::TimeUp`; the title offers *Closing Shift* against the clock and *Relaxed Run* without one, `ShiftMode` persists in the save, and the HUD counts down (amber under 5 min, red under 1). The mistake penalty now bites, because pushing `elapsed_seconds` can end the shift outright — pinned by `state/tests/shift_clock.rs`.
- **The score screen is in** (`ui/score.rs`): grade badge, toys shelved, repairs, wrong shelves, time, and a per-aisle bar table, driven by `GameSession::shift_summary`. Finishing an aisle now announces itself mid-run via `InteractionResult::Placed.completed_zone`. The HUD hides behind the panel so the score is the whole message.
- **The ~88% zone cap is now legible rather than weakened.** `ZoneProgress::broken` counts an aisle's toys currently in halves and `still_to_find()` the rest of its shortfall, so the score screen reads "41 to find - 5 to mend" and the HUD "Plush Corner - 5 to mend / 90%". An aisle with toys in pieces is genuinely not restored, so `is_restored` was left alone; what was wrong was that a player who had shelved every whole toy in an aisle had no way to tell the remainder from toys they had missed. Pinned by `every_aisle_slot_is_accounted_for_as_shelved_broken_or_missing`.
- **A shift is now worth repeating: best runs are recorded.** The game graded you and forgot — nothing carried between shifts by design, since tools are earned and lost inside one run, so a player who cleared 92% had no way to know next time whether they had beaten it. `state/records.rs` keeps a `ShiftRecord` per mode in its own save slot (`records_slot`), separate from the session because "New Game" overwrites that one and a record has to survive exactly that. The score screen reads "Best so far: 154 toys, 2 wrong, 28:32", or "New best" when the run improved it.
  - Better means **more toys, then fewer wrong shelves, then faster** — in that order, so a careful slow clear beats a scrappy quick one and speed only settles a tie. Deliberately not the grade, which is a coarse band that two different runs can share.
  - The title screen shows the record too, above the buttons that pick a mode — by the time the score screen reports one the choice has already been made, and with nothing else carrying between shifts the record is the only reason to pick the clock. Both title captions now sit on a dark plate: 15px text unbacked over a lit shop full of bright toys was barely readable, which applied to the pre-existing mode caption as well.
  - **Records never actually loaded, and the tests all passed.** `load_from_slot_with_migration` hands its callback the whole save wrapper, `{slot, data}`, not the inner payload — and every field of `BestRuns` is `serde(default)`, so deserialising the wrapper straight into it did not fail. Serde ignored the two unknown keys and returned an empty record. Written correctly, silently read back as none, every launch. The fix unwraps the envelope first, exactly as `migrate_save_value` already did for the session save; that one was never affected. Found by `on_disk::a_record_survives_a_round_trip_through_a_file`, written specifically because the disk path was the one link reasoned about rather than observed. The other two on-disk tests cover a missing file (the ordinary first run) and a corrupt one, which must cost the player their records and nothing else.
  - Timed and Relaxed keep **separate** records. A relaxed run has no deadline, so its time is not comparable; pooling them would let an unhurried perfect clear stand as *the* record and leave a Closing Shift player nothing reachable to chase.
- **The deadline is reachable.** `the_deadline_is_reachable_by_a_closer_who_buys_tools` adds `Strategy::Earner` — the only loadout a timed run ever really has, since tools do not carry between shifts — and it clears the shop in 20.0 min against the 30 min deadline, versus 27.9 bare-handed. Ten minutes of slack for a near-optimal closer that teleports in straight lines and never backtracks, so a real player should find 30 minutes comfortable played well and tight played carelessly. Still worth a human playthrough, since the replay's 0.6s-per-interaction constant is untuned.

## Validation

- **The end-to-end publish runs clean.** `.\publish.ps1` (preview target, not production) builds Windows and WebGL, packs `assets.zip`, renders `index.html` from `game_page.json`, and deploys to `D:\xampp\htdocs\games\toybox`. The wasm links at 1.7 MB, the generated page carries the `Q`-key controls row added with the trolley fix, and its scripts resolve to `shared-assets/runtime/` rather than per-game copies.
- **Not yet verified in an actual browser.** The Chrome extension was not connected, so nothing above proves the page *boots* — only that it builds, packages and deploys. Loading `http://127.0.0.1/games/toybox/` by hand is the outstanding check, and the one place a WASM-only failure (asset pack, storage bridge, WebGL context) would show up. **Retried since; the Chrome extension is still not connected**, so this stays open and is the one item here that cannot be closed from inside the repo.
- **The player's save now round-trips through a real file, and so does an old one.** Every migration test called `migrate_save_value` on a `Value` built in memory — which is not the code that runs. A save written by this build carries the current version, so `load_from_slot_with_migration` takes its *fast path* and deserialises the wrapper directly, never calling `migrate_save_value` at all; the migration suite could not reach it. That is the same blind spot that hid the records bug for two iterations, on the more important path. `on_disk::a_session_survives_a_round_trip_through_a_file` writes a played session (shelved, mis-shelved, carrying, repaired, a tool bought) to a scratch game name in app data and reads it back, and fails outright if the migration callback runs at all — a save this build just wrote must carry this build's version. A companion test writes a 2.1.0 save and checks it really does route through migration. **Both passed first time: the session save was correct, and is now verified rather than assumed.**
- Deploying surfaced a save-migration gap worth keeping: `strip_post_expansion_fields` did not strip `player.repairs` or `shift_mode`, so no test exercised a save written before the score screen and the deadline. Those saves are sitting in real players' localStorage. Now stripped, with the load asserting Timed mode and a shift that has not already ended.

## Polish

- **Screenshot captures were not reproducible, which is why staleness could only ever be caught by eye.** The harness simulates a fixed number of frames at a fixed timestep precisely so a capture repeats — but four `get_time()` calls (a lamp breathing, dust motes, completion sparkles, the scanner beacon) read macroquad's wall clock, so the animation phase depended on how long the process took to reach the capture frame. The same scene captured twice differed in bytes, so no drift check could ever tell a stale reference image from a fresh one. `ui::animation_seconds()` now accumulates the same `dt` the simulation gets; `rendering_animates_off_the_simulation_clock` keeps the wall clock out of `src/ui` and `src/toys`.
  - Seven of nine scenes went from varying to byte-identical across repeated captures. **Two still vary** — `mid_run` and `carrying_a_half_scanned` — by ~109 pixels out of 860,784 (0.013%), confined to anti-aliased text edges in the HUD panel, with channel deltas up to 39/255. Nothing in this repo reads a clock any more, the spatial grid sorts so draw order is fixed, and raising the capture from 30 to 150 frames does not settle it — so it is not animation phase and not a framebuffer settle-in. It looks like font-atlas rasterisation in the shared toolkit; still unproven, but no longer in the way.
- **Capture drift is detectable now: `.\scripts\check-captures.ps1`.** It re-captures all 67 references — the 17 scenes *and* the 50-toy gallery whose staleness started this — into gitignored `dist\capture-check`, compares against the committed images without overwriting them, and exits non-zero on drift. A hash cannot work for the scenes because of the wobble above, so it measures the **percentage of differing pixels**, which separates the failure modes cleanly: noisy scenes read 0.001–0.055%, everything else reads 0.000%, and a genuinely stale reference measured **3.159%** — the check caught one on its first run, an image accidentally left behind from a 150-frame experiment.
  - The two sets get different thresholds because only one is byte-reproducible. Scenes: 0.1%. Toys: **0.01%**, since they are exact and the tighter gate buys real sensitivity — a controlled test moving one primitive in the bear's model by 0.06 registers as 0.179%, which a 0.1% gate only just catches. Verified both ways: the perturbed bear was flagged while the untouched octopus read 0.000%, and it went green again once reverted.
- **The 50-toy reference gallery was three weeks stale, and the sweep that refreshes it had gone blind.** Every image in `docs/verification/toys/` dated from before two commits that touched the toy renderers, so all fifty differed from what the game draws. Worse, `capture_toys.ps1` built its list by listing `src/toys/*.rs` and subtracting a hardcoded denylist of helper modules — adding `part_accents.rs` made it sweep a non-toy, and the harness dutifully rendered a bear captioned "UNKNOWN TOY" which the script reported as ok, 52/52. The list now derives from the `ToyIdentity::X => x::draw` dispatch, so helpers are excluded for free and a new toy is picked up once it can actually be drawn. All fifty re-swept; spot-checked across plushies, board games, robots and dragons, and nothing was broken — only out of date.
- **Broken *bodies* had ears.** The lineup scene only ever forced heads, so the five body renderers — half of everything `part_accents.rs` touches — had never been seen. They were drawing the same crest, which put rabbit ears and antennae on a headless torso, floating above the neck stump. A crest is a head feature; bodies now vary their own limbs by the same `accent_scale` instead (chunkier on an Elephant, compact on a Cat), which is a body-shaped cue and keeps head and body visibly different halves. `crest_scale` renamed to `accent_scale` to match what it now drives, and `TOYBOX_CAPTURE_PART_KIND=body` makes the other half capturable.
- **Two HUD faults found by capturing the scenes nobody had looked at.** `checkout` showed a zone with no displays drawing a *full* progress bar under the words "no shelves" — `fraction()` returns 1.0 there so aggregates do not treat an empty zone as outstanding work, but rendered as a meter it claims a finished aisle. No bar is drawn now. `lamp_contrast` showed "Dragon Alcove - 7 to mend" spilling past the panel edge onto the shop floor: the label suffix added with the repair count only ever fit because "Plush Corner" is short. The panel widened to 250 and the label is fitted, so a longer zone name truncates inside the box instead of escaping it.
- **Both shift modes drew the identical clock, and one counts the other way.** Every capture scene was a timed shift, so relaxed mode's HUD had never been rendered. Put beside `mid_run` it is pixel-identical but for the digits — same stopwatch icon, same colour, same position — yet `29:59` is time *left* and `07:11` is time *gone*. The mode was named exactly once, in a notification at the start of the run that fades, which is the same shape of gap as `Q` and the trolley: stated at the moment you already knew, then never again. It got worse the moment quitting started saving, since a player can now resume a shift days later having forgotten which mode it was.
  - The clock now carries a right-aligned `LEFT` / `ELAPSED` caption, muted so it does not compete with the digits when they redden. Right-aligned off the measured width rather than placed after them: the countdown is five characters and a long relaxed run passes six.
  - `ShiftMode::clock_caption` derives the word from `shows_countdown` rather than restating it, because a caption that disagreed with the direction is worse than none. `the_clock_caption_says_which_way_the_clock_runs` pins the *implication* both ways, plus that the two words differ at all.
  - **The drift check passed this and should not have.** The caption changed eight scenes by 0.019% each — real and deliberate, but under the 0.1% gate, which cannot be lowered past the ~0.055% noise floor from font rasterisation. A small HUD addition is exactly the change that fits underneath. All eight were re-captured by hand; `CLAUDE.md` now says to re-capture every scene drawing the surface you touched instead of waiting for the check to name them. It is a backstop against forgetting, not an inventory of what changed.
- **The pause menu threw the shift away, and it was the only way out of a run.** The Settings panel had never been captured in either of its two forms — from the title, and mid-shift as the pause menu, which is not the same screen with a different heading (it adds a second button and lays its row out to a different width). Rendering them found that "Quit to Title" wrote nothing: `Ctrl+S` was the *sole* path to the save slot, so leaving a run discarded it and the title then offered "Continue" backed by whatever older save happened to be on disk, with nothing to say the shift just abandoned was gone. Leaving now saves, and the button reads **Save & Quit** in a muted tone — the Danger red said the opposite of what it now does.
  - Only while the shift is still playable. A finished run must not overwrite a live one, or "Continue" resumes onto a score screen for a shift that already ended and was already recorded. The rule lives on `GamePhase::should_save_on_leaving` because nothing can construct a `Game` in a test — it needs a GL context — so the caller and the test share one named rule instead of the test restating `!is_over()`.
  - `quitting_mid_shift_keeps_the_run_and_quitting_after_it_does_not` goes through a real file in app data, like the other `on_disk` tests, and asserts both halves. Verified by perturbation: forcing the rule to always-save fails it.
  - Checked while I was in there and both were fine: the shift clock is correctly frozen while paused (`update_timer` runs only on `Playing`), and the `update_bench` call that *does* run before that early return is the performance benchmark, not the repair bench.
- Every whole-store capture scene has now been inspected at least once against the current build. That is the check that keeps finding things: the tool-shop truncation, the score screen's "0 of 5 aisles restored", the robot lineup of identical boxes, and both faults above all passed a green suite first.
- **`Q` was named exactly once, at the moment of purchase.** The carried card drew a `G Drop` keycap unconditionally and a row of carry pips with the active one ringed, but nothing anywhere in play said what moved the ring. The only mention of the cycle key was in the Sorting Trolley's shop description, which a player reads once when buying it and never again — the same shape of gap as the trolley originally having no input path at all. The card now shows a `Q` keycap immediately left of the pips, and only above a carry limit of one, since a dead key on screen is worse than no key.
  - Fitting it in meant moving the pips off the name's line and down to the controls row. They had always overlapped — with one pip and a truncated name it just about read — but `Q` plus three pips ran straight through the toy's name. The name now has the card's full width to itself.
  - `carrying_armful` captures the state; every other scene shows the card in its single-toy form. **The drift check earned its keep here**: it caught `carrying_a_half_scanned` at 0.423% against a 0.055% noise floor, a scene I had changed the layout of and forgotten to re-capture.

## Engineering

- Balance the five tools against measured pacing. Over a whole 240-toy shift: bare-handed 27.9 min, trolley+scanner 20.5, fully equipped 17.6 — so a full toolset is worth about 37% of the run. The trolley now clearly pays (it used to measure *worse* than bare hands); the speed tools stack on top.
- The replay closer used to walk every toy to the **first** display of its category, ignoring the other three. That fills a quarter of the shop and then spins, carrying each remaining toy to a shelf that has been full for hours and dropping it again — 9700 wasted actions in an 8000-action shift. Every "the shop is unfinishable" number before this was measuring that, not the game. `home_display_index` now picks the nearest matching display *with room*, and `the_closer_clears_the_floor_without_misshelving` asserts the run ends because it ran out of work rather than out of budget.
- **Placement now runs through real aiming** (`aim_and_place`): the closer walks to a gap, faces it, and presses `E` only once `interaction_preview` says it will shelve — so a run is charged for putting each toy away, not just for finding it. With a trolley that is a separate walk per toy in the armful, because the slot just filled is no longer the one the crosshair offers. Doing this uncovered two `interact` ordering bugs and the shelf shadowing below.
- **A full front row used to shadow every row behind it.** Slots sit five to a row, so a display's capacity is its depth, and `targeted_empty_display_slot` took the best-scoring slot and then asked whether it happened to be free. Scoring favours the nearest, so once row one filled, standing at the shelf offered a taken slot and `E` refused with two empty rows a few centimetres further back. It now searches free slots only — holding a toy, the intent is unambiguous, so an occupied slot is not a candidate. Bare-handed shelf refusals went to zero at every capacity, and the 4000-toy shop went from 146 toys shelved and 0 displays completed to 3521 and 12. `a_full_front_row_does_not_block_the_rows_behind_it` pins the rule; `a_display_stays_fillable_to_its_back_row` keeps watching the shipped config.
- The "crosshair misses half the time" reading was **two different things added together**, and the report now splits them:
  - *Whiffed* — the crosshair delivered nothing. Bare-handed this is **zero**; the cone is fine. What produced 175 whiffs was the Sorting Trolley having no input path at all (fixed: `E` on a loose toy now loads the armful when there is room). The 66 that remain are the closer aiming at a toy that sits in front of a free shelf slot, where `E` correctly shelves instead — real friction, but the right precedence.
  - *Neighbour* — the crosshair handed over a different toy than the one aimed at, ~175–195 of 400. The closer still walks away holding a toy, so this is pile texture rather than lost work, and it is arguably the point of a floor buried in toys. Leaving as-is.
